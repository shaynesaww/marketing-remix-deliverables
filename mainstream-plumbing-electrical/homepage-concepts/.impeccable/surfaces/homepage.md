## 1. What this is

**Mechanism.** One licensed man, paid hourly, who arrives once and fixes both the water and the wire, and charges the same at two in the morning as two in the afternoon.

**Audience scene.** Two scenes, not one. A kitchen at 1:47am with water spreading across the floor and a phone in one hand. And a kitchen table in daylight with three quotes fanned out, thousands of dollars apart, and a homeowner trying to work out which one is lying.

**Cultural home.** The Carolina Upstate. Red clay that stains everything it touches. Locate flags in a front yard. A Rheem tank in a hall closet. An F-150 at the curb. The kitchen table itself.

## 2. The rut, named and excluded

**The category default:** a blue gradient hero, a stock photograph of a smiling technician in a spotless uniform giving a thumbs up, a "24/7 EMERGENCY SERVICE" starburst, three icon cards reading Licensed / Insured / Affordable, and a row of five gold stars.

**Its predictable opposite:** the all-white minimal startup look. Thin geometric sans, enormous whitespace, one pastel accent, soft-shadow cards, a lifestyle photo of nobody in particular.

Neither is available. The first is what every competitor in Greenville already looks like. The second is what a designer reaches for when trying not to look like the first, and it would read as a software company rather than a man with a truck.

## 3. Seven grounded visual systems, ordered by resonance

Drawn from the audience's actual world, spanning five material families.

| # | System | Material family |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Utility locate marking.** The APWA uniform color code sprayed on grass and clay before anyone digs: blue for water, red for electric, orange for comms, wire flags on stems. A real codified color language that happens to map exactly onto this company's two trades. | Paint on earth, wire, stencil |
| 2 | **The kitchen table.** His own sentence made physical. Oak or formica, a coffee ring, an itemized invoice laid flat, a ballpoint pen. | Wood, paper, ink |
| 3 | **The carbon-copy service ticket.** Numbered, ruled, boxed fields, a price written in by hand, a signature line, two-color print that occasionally misregisters. | Paper, print, ruled grid |
| 4 | **Red clay in section.** The exposed trench wall, orange strata against green grass. The single most distinctive material fact of doing this work in the Upstate, and it is present in both real work photographs. | Earth, photography |
| 5 | **The panel directory.** A dead front, a handwritten circuit card, breaker positions in a strict two-column grid, label tape. | Painted steel, label tape |
| 6 | **The riser diagram.** Isometric plumbing and circuit runs, copper and PEX schedules, the line-drawing language of a plumbing plan. | Vector line drawing |
| 7 | **The wrap.** Cyan, lime diagonals, water texture, "RAPID RESPONSE UNIT," the unit number 22. | Vinyl, vehicle graphic |

## 4. The seven directions

**D1 Locate Marks** · the marking code as the whole system. High-chroma blue and red on a dark clay ground, sprayed rules and stencil condensed caps as section dividers, wire-flag accents. Drenched color strategy.
**D2 The Table** · warm oak and paper, a real serif, the page composed as objects laid on a surface. Quiet, zero chrome.
**D3 Work Order** · the service ticket as the page grid. Ruled boxes, field labels in small caps, monospace figures, navy and green in two-color register. Restrained.
**D4 Wrap** · lean all the way into the vehicle. Cyan, lime diagonals, italic condensed display, a unit-number system. Loud.
**D5 Red Clay** · photography-led and earth-toned. The clay cut as the dominant surface, strata bands as section breaks, a heavy grotesque, brand color used sparingly as the only saturated thing on the page.
**D6 Panel Directory** · a strict labeled two-column grid on painted steel gray, every section a numbered slot.
**D7 Riser Diagram** · blueprint navy, isometric line runs connecting sections, hairline rules, a drafting-plate type pairing.

## 5. What the roll assigned

**Index 3 = D3 Work Order.**

The assignment is the roll, not a suggestion, and it earns the slot on grounds beyond the dice. The brand's own advice to a homeowner is *ask for it itemized, and if a quote will not itemize, that tells you something.* A world built from the itemized ticket is the positioning rendered as a surface rather than asserted in a sentence. It also has somewhere honest to put $78: a price box with a figure written in it.

## 6. The three that get built

The project owner promised this client three homepage variations, and that brief wins over the skill's convergence on one. The dealt hand is used the way it was meant to be used, to refuse the model's ranking rut, and then three are taken from it with genuine spread rather than three shades of one idea.

| Variant | Direction | First viewport strategy | Buyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **D1 Locate Marks** | Emergency and price. Phone at maximum scale, $78, same at 2am, on site within 90 minutes. | The one standing in water |
| **B** | **D5 Red Clay** | Owner proof. The man face down in the clay, nobody works on commission. | The planned job, already burned once |
| **C** | **D3 Work Order** *(assigned)* | Scope and single visit. Both trades as line items on one document. | The head term, 1,780/mo |

**Spread check.** Material families: paint on earth / photograph of earth / paper and print. Color strategy: drenched / committed with restraint / two-color restrained. Type: stencil condensed / heavy grotesque / monospace with small caps. Ground: dark / photographic / light paper. No two of these could be mistaken for each other in a thumbnail, which is the whole point of showing three.

**Rejected from the hand, and why.** D2 The Table shares paper with D3 and warmth with D5, so it would have read as a fourth shade rather than a third option. D4 Wrap reproduces an existing amateur asset. D6 and D7 are both rigid grid systems that would have collided with D3.

## 7. Implementation fidelity

No image generation is available this session and none is wanted: the client rejected AI imagery outright. Every visual role resolves as follows.

| Region | Medium |
|---|---|
| Hero photography, owner portraits, truck | **Raster.** Real photographs, already owned, optimized to WebP with JPG fallback. |
| Logo mark and wordmark | **Vector.** Redrawn as SVG from the JPG source, because both source files sit on white and at least one world runs dark. |
| Section dividers, marking sprays, strata bands, ruled boxes | **CSS.** Geometry and hard-edged shape systems, no texture faked as a gradient. |
| Service and utility icons | **Inline SVG**, drawn to match each world. No icon library, no emoji, no unicode glyphs. |
| Motion | **CSS only.** GoHighLevel will not carry anything more. |
| Accepted omission | No photograph of the owner's face exists. No world depends on one. |
