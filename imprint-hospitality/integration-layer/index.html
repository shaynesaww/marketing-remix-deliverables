The gap today

Where the time goes

The same room block, typed three times

The system in the main proposal handles the selling. Leads, proposals, contracts, signatures, payments, the portal, the dashboards. What it cannot do on its own is stay in agreement with the system your properties actually run on.

Right now a person fills that gap, and that person is usually you. The same room block gets typed into the CRM, then into the contract, then into StayNTouch. Pickup gets checked by opening StayNTouch and looking. Commissions get rebuilt every month in a spreadsheet, from a report pulled property by property, at a different rate for each one.

None of that is broken, exactly. It just costs a person several hours a month per property and it produces a number nobody can independently check.

What this layer is, in one sentence

It pulls each property's data out of StayNTouch, does the calculations in one place, and writes the results into HighLevel. Blocks, pickup, room revenue and commissions all come off that one path. Sitting on top of it is a write path running the other way, so a signed contract creates its block in StayNTouch without anyone retyping it.

The design principle this whole thing follows

Runtime

A service on managed infrastructure that scales with load. Nightly runs and month-end reconciliation hit every property at once and then it sits idle, which is exactly the shape this should be. No servers for anyone to patch.

Database

One managed Postgres holding configuration, sync state, calculated figures and the audit trail, with automated daily backups and a restore procedure tested during the build rather than assumed.

Why a service, not a workflow tool

Two things here carry real consequences: creating live inventory holds, and producing a number that gets billed. A service lets the commission logic sit under an automated test that runs a known month and asserts the answer to the cent. That is the difference between saying the math is right and showing it.

What it does

Diagram five

Six jobs, and any one of them can wait

Each is built as its own piece, so it can be sequenced, deferred or dropped without disturbing the others. The first three only read. The two marked as writing are the ones that reach into a live hotel, and they are built accordingly. Click any of them.

Property setup syncReads only  ·  re-runnable any time

What starts it

Onboarding a property, and then any time that property changes its setup. You can re-run it on demand, so a rate change at the property never means a rebuild here.

What moves, and which way

Room types, rate structures including seasonal and day-of-week variation, tax rules, deposit and cancellation policies, and hold statuses. StayNTouch into HighLevel, one direction only.

What you see in HighLevel

Every room type and rate sitting on the property record as a ready-to-use value, so a proposal builds from real numbers and nobody types a room type by hand again.

Done when

A property is onboarded without anyone typing a room type, and what shows in HighLevel matches StayNTouch exactly.

PickupReads only  ·  on-demand button plus a nightly run

What starts it

A button on the room block record when you want it now, and a scheduled run every night so the number is never more than a day old.

What moves, and which way

StayNTouch reports contracted, picked up and remaining directly, per room type per night. This reads that and brings back the reservation-level detail too: who booked, which room type, which dates, what status.

What you see in HighLevel

Picked up per room type, the total, what is remaining, the percentage, and when it last refreshed. This is the piece TripleSeat cannot match, because it only ever receives a room count back and you get the reservations behind it.

Done when

Pickup in HighLevel matches StayNTouch for a block with real reservations against it.

Actualized revenue & commissionsReads and calculates  ·  monthly, plus on demand

What starts it

A monthly run at close, plus on demand whenever you want to look early. This is the flow that replaces the spreadsheet.

What moves, and which way

StayNTouch reports revenue broken out by charge type, so room revenue separates cleanly from tax, food and beverage, and fees. Commission is owed on room revenue only, which is what makes that separation the entire point.

What you see in HighLevel

Room revenue summed per block, rolled up per property per month, with that property's rate applied. Rates are stored as editable values with effective dates, because they differ per property and they change.

Done when

The agreed method, applied to a month you have already billed, matches your figure to the cent.

Block creation on signatureWrites to StayNTouch  ·  the redundancy that costs the most time

What starts it

A contract reaching fully signed in HighLevel. Nothing before that point touches your inventory.

What moves, and which way

The client account, the block, room types and counts per night, rates, cutoff date, deposit and cancellation terms, hold status, and the booking code your guests use. HighLevel into StayNTouch.

What you see in HighLevel

The StayNTouch identifiers come straight back onto the record, so the contract and the block are linked from the moment the block exists rather than matched up later.

Done when

A signed contract produces the right block in StayNTouch, checked by eye against the interface, and a retry after a failure does not create a second one.

Amendments, cancellations & driftWrites to StayNTouch  ·  compares both directions nightly

What starts it

A change to a signed contract, a cancellation, or the nightly comparison run that comes as standard.

What moves, and which way

Revised counts, revised rates, or a cancellation that releases the rooms, each with a note recorded against the block so the history is on the record rather than in somebody's inbox.

What you see in HighLevel

Your property staff will edit blocks directly in StayNTouch, and that is fine. Every night this compares what StayNTouch holds against what the contract says and flags any difference, showing both values. Nothing is silently overwritten in either direction. A person decides which one is right.

Done when

An amended block updates, a cancelled booking releases the rooms, and a direct edit at the property is surfaced rather than lost.

Availability lookupReads only  ·  on demand, before the quote

What starts it

A button on the booking record, pressed before a proposal goes out.

What moves, and which way

A live check against StayNTouch for the dates and room types being asked about. Read-only, and it holds nothing.

What you see in HighLevel

Whether the rooms exist for those dates, before you quote them rather than after you have already promised them.

Done when

The lookup returns real availability for a real date range and matches what StayNTouch shows on screen.

Property eleven

Multi-property

Adding a property is a record, not a rebuild

Onboarding the eleventh property should not look anything like building the first. That is a design decision made at the start, and it is the reason this is a single service rather than ten connected accounts.

Every property lives in the same database as a configuration record: its StayNTouch identifier, its credentials, its commission rate with an effective date, its sync schedule, and who gets the alerts. The engine reads that record and contains no property-specific logic anywhere in it.

On credentials and separation

Credentials are held encrypted, and each property's data is isolated at the query level, so no property can see another one's figures. That matters more here than in most builds, because your properties are separately owned and their commission rates are not each other's business.

Your question about StayNTouch going away, answered structurally

You asked what happens if a property moves off StayNTouch. This is the design answer. The database, the duplicate guard, the calculation engine and the alerting are all shared and none of them know what a PMS is. Only the piece that speaks StayNTouch is StayNTouch-specific, so adding a second property system later means rebuilding that one piece against new endpoints. Everything above it stays untouched. The same is true for your two Cloudbeds properties whenever you want them brought in.

The money

How the number gets proved

Matching a month you have already billed

This is the flow that justifies the project on its own, and it is also the one where trust me is not a good enough answer. So it does not rely on trust.

Replacing a spreadsheet with an automated figure is only an improvement if the figure is right, and the honest problem with automated money math is that it is confidently wrong in ways a person reading a report will not catch. A commission that is off by half a percent looks exactly like a commission that is correct.

So the number gets proved before it ever gets used, in four steps:

  1. I write a commission-method specification before any code exists. Which revenue counts, how rounding works, how tax is treated, and the rate and effective date for each property.
  2. You and your CEO sign it off. It is short, it is in plain language, and it is the one document in this project worth reading twice.
  3. That specification is then encoded as an automated test, run against a month you have already billed and closed.
  4. The test runs again on every change to the system afterwards, so the math cannot quietly drift six months from now.

The acceptance standard, stated plainly

The agreed method, applied to a previously-billed month, matches your figure to the cent. If it does not match because the original had a manual error in it, I flag that and show you where. I do not reproduce a mistake in order to pass my own test.

Rates are configuration, never code

Commission rates differ per property and they change over time, so they are stored as editable values carrying effective dates. That means a rate change is something you can see, change, and date, rather than a support request to me. It also means last year's figures still recalculate at last year's rate.

When it breaks

Failure handling

It holds live rooms, so it assumes it will fail

This layer creates real inventory holds at your properties and calculates money you bill to your owners. Everything below is part of the build rather than an upgrade on top of it.

No duplicate blocks, structurally

Every block creation carries a unique fingerprint enforced by the database itself, not by a check in code that two simultaneous runs can race past. A retry after a failed or half-finished attempt recognises the block already exists and refuses to make a second one.

A full audit trail

Every attempt, payload, response and calculated figure is stored with a timestamp. When a block and a contract disagree eight weeks from now, the record of what was sent and when already exists and nobody has to reconstruct it.

Alerts a person actually sees

Anything on the write path or the money path notifies immediately, to whoever you name for that property. Everything else waits for the daily summary.

A manual path for everything

Documented, so a failure is workable the same day by a person rather than a blocked booking waiting on me.

Conservative pacing

All StayNTouch traffic is deliberately throttled. This integration is never going to be the reason a property tells you their system is slow.

Runs that report themselves

A nightly run reports its own outcome, which means a run that never happened raises an alert too. Silence is not treated as success.

One decision I need from you on this

If a block gets created in StayNTouch but the rates come back rejected, there are two reasonable ways to handle it. Roll the whole thing back, or keep the block, mark it incomplete, and alert immediately. I recommend the second, because a block with the wrong rates is fixable in minutes, while a rolled-back block in the middle of a signature flow means rooms that were never actually held. If you would rather have it the other way, say so and I will build it that way.

The calendar

Four weeks of build, inside the wave-two window

Foundation, read, write, prove

The dates in the main proposal do not change. Core system live November 1, integration proven on Kinship around mid-December, remaining properties in January. This is what happens inside that window.

Week 1
Foundation and access. Service, database and deployment stood up, and the property configuration model built. The field contract at the seam gets agreed and locked before the HighLevel schema freezes, because several of those decisions are hard to reverse once records exist. The StayNTouch certification and credential request goes in on day one, so that waiting runs alongside the build instead of in front of it.
Week 2
Read and calculate. Setup sync and pickup running against the test environment. Both are read-only, so they can proceed while live credentials are still being issued. The commission-method specification gets drafted and put in front of you.
Week 3
Write path and money. Block creation with the duplicate guard. Amendments, cancellations and drift detection. The commission engine built to the signed specification, with the historical-month test passing. Alerting and audit trail live.
Week 4
Pilot on Kinship, with real contracts. Every flow verified against StayNTouch by eye. A month you have already billed, reconciled against what this produces. Failures deliberately forced, to confirm the alerts actually fire rather than assuming they will.
Mid-Dec
Live on Kinship

Then, in January

Remaining properties get added as configuration records, each one verified before it goes live. That rollout is paced by how fast credentials are issued, not by how fast anything gets built, which is why it sits after the pilot rather than alongside it.

The one dependency I do not control

StayNTouch has to issue credentials and certify the integration. They charge nothing for it and they advertise it as fast, but it is their calendar and not mine. That is exactly why the read paths get built first and against test data: if access runs late, weeks one through three are unaffected and only the pilot moves. It is the same reasoning that made the integration wave two rather than wave one. That risk sits next to your November date, never underneath it.

The short version

A number you can check to the cent

The room block gets typed once, by the system, at the moment the contract is signed. Pickup sits on the record without anyone opening a second tab to find it. And on the first of the month the commission figure is already calculated, from the revenue StayNTouch itself reported, at the rate you set for that property.

The last one is what I would judge this on. Moving data between two systems is not hard and plenty of people will tell you they can do it. The real test is whether the number that comes out matches the one you already billed, and that is the thing I am willing to put into the acceptance criteria rather than into a sales conversation.

Which is why the specification gets signed before the code gets written, and why the test runs against a month you have already closed. If it does not match, it is not done.
