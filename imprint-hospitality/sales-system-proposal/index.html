The design decision underneath all of this

Reporting is the part of any CRM that quietly fails eighteen months in, because the numbers were stored somewhere that cannot be totalled. So room revenue and site fees get captured as real, separate numbers on the booking records themselves, and property and event type get set up as selectable values rather than typed text. That sounds like a technical detail. It is the entire difference between these reports existing and not, and it is why it happens in week two rather than as an afterthought in October.

The compromises, named

Conversations attach to the client, not to the booking

This is the single biggest difference from TripleSeat and the one you will notice first. TripleSeat threads sixty-seven emails onto one booking. Here, the thread lives on the contact. In practice one client rarely runs two bookings at once, so the contact thread is the booking thread. We stamp booking references into email subjects and keep notes on the booking itself. I would rather you hear this from me now than discover it in October.

No room inventory engine

Nothing on this platform holds room availability. You already approved the answer: check the property calendar by hand, the way you do today. The system holds the block once it exists, it just does not know what is free.

No forward-looking money reporting

The platform reports on money it has collected. It has no way to report on money that is scheduled but not yet paid, which is why the cash forecast came out of scope rather than onto the list. What replaces it: a live list of what is owed and when, and reminders that chase the client automatically at every due date. You lose the chart and keep most of the outcome. I found this by checking rather than assuming, and it is the one thing on this proposal that got smaller after I verified it.

No multi-state tax engine

Colorado, California and Vermont all tax differently. Each property carries its own rates as stored values that fill the proposal automatically. Your approval gate is what catches an error before a client sees it.

The money math is assisted, not automatic, in version one

Rates, taxes and service charges are entered and verified by a person at proposal time. The rate intelligence engine you described — the one that reads Lighthouse and quotes for you — is Phase 3, and it needs a year of your own booking data behind it to be worth building.

Very large deposits may need splitting

Bank transfers carry per-transaction limits. On a large deposit that means either splitting the payment or taking a card. Worth knowing before it happens, not during.
